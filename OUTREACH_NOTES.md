# Outreach notes: factual talking points

Not to be sent as-is. Use for drafting.

## Ground rules for every audience

- The project is independent and non-partisan, and uses public records only.
- Cases are **investigation leads**, not findings. Each comes with legitimate explanations and the specific record that would settle it.
- Lead with method and verifiability, not with the anomalies.
- Use the neutral phrasing: "no explaining approval was found in the public records examined".
- Mention what was ruled out. It is the strongest credibility signal.
- **Offer:**
  - the data under attribution;
  - corrections welcome;
  - the method to extend to other areas.

## Journalists

- **Investigation-ready packs.** Five case files, each on one page:
  - what was promised and what happened;
  - why it merits follow-up and possible legitimate explanations;
  - questions to ask, and every source linked with page number and URL.
- **Independent verification.** Every figure links to a BBMP IFMS or KPPP record, or a public attachment. You can check a case in minutes without trusting the tool.
- **The strongest lead: the Ejipura–Sony World elevated corridor.**
  - ₹160.7 Cr paid.
  - Two extensions on the original contract. The balance contract was due February 2025: no extension is visible and there is no final bill.
  - ₹15 Cr of bills pending; penalties stated in orders (₹1.58 Cr) differ from fines deducted (₹31 L).
  - The specific RTI requests are drafted.
- **The resolved signals are a story in themselves.** Several dramatic early flags (paid 220%, double-billed equipment, shared contracts) were explained by documents already in the public record. That says something about how hard the record is to read.
- **Other Bengaluru areas can be processed.** The same pipeline runs per area from a config file. An HSR Layout demo found 549 paid bills from the cached city-wide grid with no new collection.

## Civic groups and BBMP

- **Lifecycle visibility.** For 432 Koramangala works:
  - the plan (estimate, sanction);
  - the contract (work order, agreement);
  - the work documented (bill forms, MB, completion certificate);
  - the payments;
  - the completion record.
- **Documentation gaps, stated neutrally:**
  - Measurement books are mostly handwritten scans (8 of 144 machine-readable).
  - Some MB and completion slots are filled with "Not Applicable" placeholder PDFs.
  - Bill-level attachments for bills from mid-2024 are not publicly visible.
  - Work slips are sometimes filed under unexpected names.
- **Auditability suggestions** that would make the public record self-explaining. Each comes from a specific failure seen in the data:
  - typed bill abstracts;
  - consistent attachment types;
  - work-order ↔ tender references;
  - extension orders attached to the bill they affect.
- **Public-data quality.** Job numbers repeat across the 198-, 225- and 243-ward maps, and there is no tender ↔ job register. Both make independent tracking error-prone.
- **Reproducibility.** Open code, a rate-limited and cached collector, and tests. Anyone can rebuild the dataset.
- **Corrections.** If a case is explained by a document not in the public portal, sharing it closes the case publicly.

## Political research teams

- **Ward and constituency reconstruction.** Works can be grouped by ward under each delimitation, with money paid, contractors, timelines and documentation status, all source-linked.
- **Evidence-backed questions.** The RTI file lists specific records (job number, item, date) rather than broad requests. The same format suits questions in council or assembly.
- **Extensible.** Adding an area means writing a config: name spellings, ward numbers per map, sub-localities. No pipeline changes are needed. The method is the same for every area, so comparisons are like-for-like.
- **Neutral by design.**
  - Thresholds are stated, and the case score is a transparent sum used only for prioritisation.
  - Signals that were explained are published alongside open ones.
  - The data supports questions, not conclusions about any party or person.

## What not to say

- That any case proves wrongdoing, or that money was "siphoned", "lost" or "stolen".
- That a missing public attachment means a document doesn't exist.
- Anything partisan, or anything that singles out individuals.
