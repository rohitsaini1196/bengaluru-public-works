Limitations

  • The paid-bill denominator is reliable only from mid-2015. Older bills come from published registers whose completeness is unknown.
  • Unpaid bills may be incomplete. The payment grid lists only paid bills; bills stuck at an approval level are visible only for jobs already known.
  • Newer bill attachments require a login. For bills from about mid-2024, IFMS shows no bill-level attachments (MB, bill form) without a session, and none were sought.
  • Absence from public attachments does not prove a document does not exist. Approvals may exist on paper. Cases say "no explaining approval was found in the public records examined" for this reason.
  • Handwritten measurement books are usually not machine-readable, so what was measured on site is mostly unverified.
  • OCR can fail. Quantities are used only when the printed arithmetic checks out; values read from scans are marked inferred.
  • No GPS on site photos. Locations come from work descriptions, not photos.
  • Administrative documents do not prove physical existence or quality. A completion certificate is not a site inspection.
  • Job numbers and ward maps are complex. The same job number can belong to different wards on the 198-, 225- and 243-ward maps; scope rules guard against this but can miss works described only by road names, or booked to zonal or departmental codes.
  • Tender ↔ bill links are inferred. No public register maps tenders to job numbers; links carry a confidence and their evidence.
